# Visual — Deep Research: Feature Value, Workflow, and Competitive UX

## Executive findings and evidence basis

The evidence points away from building a smaller ZoomText or SuperNova and toward a more specific proposition: **make magnification spatially stable across a known two- or three-display workspace**.

The strongest opportunity is not simply “better zoom”. It is the combination of:

**independent monitor state + persistent overview/detail + fast temporary enlargement + reliable pointer/focus finding + simple monitor roles/workspace recall.**

That combination attacks the main structural weakness of conventional magnification: once the image is enlarged, field of view disappears and the software must continually decide what portion of the desktop the user should see. ZoomText and SuperNova solve this through increasingly elaborate tracking, views, freeze/hooked regions and multi-monitor modes. With several physical displays, Doxa can potentially keep more context physically present instead of continually moving a virtual viewport. ZoomText's own “Zoom with 1x” mode explicitly uses one magnified display and one unmagnified display to provide a bird's-eye view, while SuperNova provides side-by-side, clone and presentation arrangements. citeturn3view1turn9view0

There is unusually strong validation for the underlying multi-monitor problem. Several independent low-vision users have asked for essentially the same thing: **magnify one display while leaving another at 100%**, for example so video or reference material remains visible while work is magnified. Users report Windows Magnifier's full-screen behaviour as frustrating in these configurations. These reports are anecdotes rather than prevalence data, but their consistency with the specialist-product designs is significant. citeturn17search3turn17search7turn17search15turn17search19

There is an additional Doxa-specific opening: the current ZoomText documentation says multiple-monitor support currently works with **two extended displays** and that configurations with three or more extended displays are not supported. That does not prove demand for three-screen magnification, but it means a Doxa triple-screen configuration is not simply a commodity use case already comprehensively covered by ZoomText. citeturn4view0

At the other end of the market, PowerToys independently validates several of the same problems among mainstream Windows users. Find My Mouse, Mouse Jump, crosshairs and CursorWrap all address finding or travelling with the pointer on large/multiple desktops; FancyZones addresses persistent spatial organisation; Workspaces captures and relaunches groups of applications into saved positions. These are not accessibility products, which makes their existence particularly useful evidence for mainstream overlap. citeturn15view0turn15view1turn15view2

### What the evidence does and does not establish

I use four distinctions throughout:

**Observed capability** means it is documented by the product maker. **User evidence** means a user independently described a need, benefit or failure. **Inference** is the commercial/product conclusion drawn from those sources. **Research gap** means the public evidence is too weak to claim actual usage frequency or preference.

One important gap concerns magnification level. ZoomText supports magnification up to 60× and SuperNova up to 64×, but I found **no credible representative evidence showing the distribution of magnification levels actually used in everyday Windows work**. Maximum supported zoom is therefore a capability metric, not a feature-value metric. citeturn2view0turn7view0

Likewise, Low Vision Zoom is interesting because its current Microsoft Store proposition explicitly criticises conventional “jumping” between zoom levels and promotes smoother motion. However, its Store listing is extremely new and showed no user reviews in the indexed material. It is therefore useful as a **design signal**, not evidence that users broadly prefer its behaviour. citeturn24search6turn24search12

The direct-user evidence base is much richer for the *problem* of losing context and multi-monitor control than for the relative merits of specialist subfeatures such as image sharpening, hooked areas or advanced colour substitution. That distinction materially affects the prioritisation below.

### Magnification behaviour: what appears natural versus disorienting

The clearest conclusion is that **predictability matters more than raw smoothness**. Both established products invest heavily in automatic tracking because full-screen magnification inevitably removes context. ZoomText additionally exposes alignment choices and SuperNova provides different view geometries partly because no single automatic viewport behaviour works for every task. citeturn2view0turn8view0

Low Vision Zoom's decision to make smooth transition its central proposition is noteworthy: it suggests a plausible dissatisfaction with abrupt zoom jumps. But it is too new, and its Store evidence too thin, to conclude that animated continuous zoom should become Visual's behaviour. citeturn24search6turn24search12

The safer product hypothesis is:

**zoom should preserve the user's Point-of-Interest, movements should have obvious causes, automatic tracking should be selectively disableable, and 1× context should always be cheap to recover.**

That hypothesis is consistent with incumbent tracking controls, overview modes and direct user requests for an unzoomed second screen, but remains an inference requiring hands-on testing. citeturn3view1turn17search7turn17search19

The shortlist is driven by three different kinds of evidence.

First, **incumbent convergence** is strongest around tracking, pointer/caret/focus enhancement, colour, zoom views and multi-monitor arrangements. ZoomText and SuperNova independently devote considerable functionality to these areas. citeturn4view1turn9view0turn9view1

Third, **mainstream utilities validate the adjacent problems** without any accessibility positioning: Find My Mouse/Mouse Jump for cursor location, FancyZones for spatial roles, and Workspaces for returning applications to a known layout. citeturn15view0turn15view1turn15view2

### Migration familiarity

Visual can reduce retraining without imitating proprietary interfaces. Across incumbent systems, experienced users already understand a conceptual vocabulary of:

**zoom up/down; magnification on/off or 1×; tracking; pointer/caret/focus enhancement; colour enhancement; monitor/view selection; and keyboard-first control.** SuperNova's published hotkey reference is particularly revealing: magnification, overview, colour, visual highlighting, hooked areas, panning, monitor switching and tracking can all be operated from the keyboard. citeturn6search4

What should *not* automatically migrate is the accumulated hierarchy of Full/Overlay/Lens/Line/Docked/Clone/MultiView Local/MultiView Global and equivalent SuperNova view taxonomies. The capabilities solve real problems; the number of named modes is not itself evidence of user value. citeturn3view0turn2view0turn8view0

A plausible simplification is to expose **intent** rather than implementation vocabulary: *Overview*, *Detail*, *Reading*, *Meeting/Presentation*, *Normal*. This is an inference, but it aligns more closely with PowerToys Workspaces' named-task model than with traditional AT configuration trees. citeturn15view1

## High-value simple, difficult, and potentially avoidable capabilities

### High-value simple features

**Pointer locator and monitor locator** stand out. PowerToys already proves that on-demand spotlighting, crosshairs and multi-screen pointer jumping are useful mainstream utility concepts, while specialist products devote substantial attention to enhanced pointers. citeturn15view0turn9view1 A Doxa implementation could focus particularly on “where did my pointer go?” after a monitor transition rather than reproducing hundreds of cursor designs.

**Fast enlargement / fast 1×** also has an unusually good value-to-complexity profile at the product level. The core interaction is familiar across Windows, ZoomText and SuperNova. The UX question is preserving Point-of-Interest and avoiding disorienting movement, not adding more zoom levels. citeturn10search0turn2view0turn6search4

**Simple visual-state presets**—for example a small number of contrast/inversion/brightness states—also look attractive. Both incumbents strongly support visual enhancement, and Windows itself exposes inversion through Magnifier. The caveat is that highly personalised colour transformation can be condition-specific; simple presets have a better initial evidence/complexity balance than a full colour substitution system. citeturn8view1turn10search0

**Explicit monitor switching/boundary cues** may be similarly efficient. SuperNova's mouse buffer provides an existing specialist precedent, while PowerToys Mouse Jump/CursorWrap validates cross-monitor travel as a mainstream problem. citeturn9view0turn15view0

### High-value difficult features

**Independent per-monitor magnification** is the most strategically interesting difficult capability. ZoomText demonstrates that separate views and zoom levels are possible and useful enough to productise, but its current two-extended-display limitation is also a warning that robust topology handling is not trivial. citeturn2view0turn4view0

**Overview + Detail with seamless Point-of-Interest transfer** is probably more important than generic independent zoom. The difficult part is not drawing two scales; it is keeping the user's attention coherent when pointer, text caret, keyboard focus and application focus move. ZoomText's extensive tracking options and SuperNova's automatic tracking indicate the depth of this problem. citeturn2view0turn9view1

**Reliable caret/focus tracking without intrusive motion** is similarly high value. A professional user cannot lose Tab focus or typed text merely because the viewport is magnified; equally, a viewport that constantly jumps can become its own usability problem. Incumbent support establishes the requirement, but it does not establish the optimal Visual behaviour. citeturn4view1turn9view1

**Presentation/share-safe magnification** is potentially distinctive but technically uncertain. SuperNova's presentation mode proves the workflow exists, but public user evidence does not yet justify assuming it is common enough to absorb major early engineering effort. citeturn9view0

The strongest negative recommendation is **do not treat incumbent feature count as the requirements document**.

## Multi-monitor opportunity

The central question was:

> **Which existing magnification behaviours become substantially more useful or fundamentally different when the system knows it has two or three compact displays?**

The answer is: **the second and third displays can become persistent visual states rather than merely additional desktop acreage.**

That changes several problems fundamentally.

### Context need not follow detail

Traditional magnification has a conservation problem: as magnification rises, visible desktop area falls. Software therefore follows the mouse, caret or focus and continually moves the viewport. ZoomText and SuperNova devote major feature areas to solving the consequences. citeturn2view0turn9view1

With two compact displays, one can remain a stable overview while the other follows detail. ZoomText's own Zoom-with-1× arrangement effectively validates this pattern, and independent users explicitly ask for it. citeturn3view1turn17search7turn17search15

With **three** displays, a further separation becomes possible:

**overview/context | active magnified work | persistent reference/communication**

That specific three-role configuration is an inference rather than a documented user standard. However, it follows directly from the two-screen overview/detail need, SuperNova's side-by-side/reference concepts, and mainstream use of window/workspace tools to assign applications spatially. citeturn9view0turn15view1turn15view2

### Independent state becomes more important than maximum zoom

On one monitor, “magnification level” is primarily one number.

On several monitors, the interesting state becomes multidimensional:

**which monitor is detailed; which remains 1×; where Point-of-Interest is; which screen follows focus; what visual enhancement applies; and what should remain fixed.**

ZoomText MultiView already supports independent zoom levels and distinct monitor-local/global views. citeturn2view0 The Visual opportunity is not necessarily to expose equally complex modes, but to make those decisions **predictable from Doxa's known hardware topology**.

### Crossing monitors becomes an interaction event

Ordinary Windows treats monitor boundaries mostly as geometry. For low-vision use, crossing one may mean the pointer has disappeared onto another physical panel, focus has moved, or the active magnified view should change.

SuperNova's mouse buffer explicitly adds resistance at display boundaries; PowerToys provides Mouse Jump and CursorWrap; mainstream users report losing the cursor in three-monitor configurations. citeturn9view0turn15view0turn14search3

This suggests a distinctive Visual concept: **monitor crossing should be perceptible and optionally deliberate**, not just a coordinate change.

### A third screen is a real differentiation opportunity—but not yet a proven market requirement

ZoomText's current documentation explicitly limits multi-monitor support to two extended displays. citeturn4view0 That creates technical/product white space around triple-screen magnification.

It would be premature, however, to conclude that low-vision users are demanding triple monitors at scale. The public evidence supports *one normal + one magnified* much more strongly than it supports specific three-screen arrangements. Triple-display behaviour therefore deserves Doxa-specific user testing rather than an automatic roadmap commitment.

### Physical monitors can replace some virtual magnifier modes

Freeze View, Hooked Areas, docked views and split-screen views all have a common purpose: **keep context visible that would otherwise disappear when the viewport moves**. citeturn8view0turn8view3

A dedicated physical reference monitor could make some of these modes simpler:

Instead of “create a Hooked Area for the Excel formula bar”, a user might simply say **keep Excel/reference on the left screen**.

Instead of “enter Split Screen”, the second display itself is the persistent overview.

Instead of an elaborate Freeze View inside the magnifier, a selected region or application could remain on a dedicated reference screen.

That does not make freeze/pin functionality obsolete. It changes the relevant question from *“Can Visual reproduce Freeze View?”* to *“When is a pinned region better than assigning the source application to another display?”*

### Compact displays strengthen the case for transient rather than permanent enlargement

Doxa's 14–15in premise matters here. Raising Windows display scaling permanently increases readability but consumes limited usable area. Temporary enlargement can recover legibility **only when required**, while leaving the full screen available for structure at other times.

That gives temporary zoom stronger mainstream overlap on Doxa hardware than it would have on a 32-inch desktop monitor. This is an inference from the physical product context, not a prevalence claim.

The same is true of pointer travel: three physically small displays provide more total desktop width but also create more screen transitions. PowerToys' cross-screen pointer utilities demonstrate that this is already a mainstream utility problem before low vision is added. citeturn15view0

## Visual concepts worth testing

These are behavioural concepts for user/concept testing, not architecture or MVP selections.

### Persistent Overview + Detail

One display remains complete and unmagnified—or lightly enlarged—while a second shows a larger detail view of the user's current Point-of-Interest.

The crucial experiment is not whether users like two images. It is **how detail should follow the overview**: mouse, keyboard focus, text caret, explicit command, or some mixture.

This has the strongest evidence base because ZoomText implements essentially the same high-level idea and multiple low-vision users independently ask for it. citeturn3view1turn17search7turn17search15turn17search19

### Inspect and Return

The user invokes enlargement for a control, paragraph, spreadsheet cell, diagram or small UI element; the relevant region enlarges without destroying surrounding context; releasing/toggling the command restores exactly the prior 1× state.

The concept combines familiar zoom controls with Low Vision Zoom's attempt to make transitions less abrupt, but should test **immediacy against smoothness** rather than assuming animation is intrinsically better. citeturn24search6turn6search4

For normal-vision Doxa users this may be more commercially interesting than persistent magnification: it becomes “inspect small content on a compact monitor”.

### Find and Move

An on-demand command makes the pointer unmistakable and indicates which monitor contains it. A second action can deliberately send it to a specified monitor, ideally without requiring a long physical mouse sweep.

PowerToys already demonstrates both halves—Find My Mouse and Mouse Jump—and SuperNova validates deliberate monitor switching/boundary handling for magnification users. citeturn15view0turn9view0

This is probably the cleanest cross-over concept between accessibility and ordinary three-monitor productivity.

### Follow What Matters

Visual can visually identify mouse pointer, text caret and keyboard focus independently. The user chooses which of these causes the magnified detail view to move.

That separation is important. ZoomText and SuperNova both distinguish the underlying targets, while their breadth of tracking settings indicates that “always follow everything” is not an adequate model. citeturn4view1turn9view1

The concept-test question should be: **When should Visual observe, highlight, follow or ignore each target?**

### Monitor Roles

The Doxa screens are assigned stable purposes such as:

**Overview | Work/Detail | Reference/Communication**

or, for two screens:

**Work/Detail | Context**

Applications and visual states return to those roles after reconnecting the Doxa system or entering a named work mode.

PowerToys FancyZones and Workspaces provide strong mainstream evidence for saved spatial organisation, though Workspaces' documented application-positioning exceptions warn against promising perfect restoration. citeturn15view1turn15view2

The novelty is combining workspace role with **visual state**, rather than merely window coordinates.

### Fixed Reference

The user sends either a region or an entire application to a reference display and tells Visual not to let focus/magnification activity displace it.

Likely workflows include spreadsheet formula/result comparison, browser reference material while writing, an email/task list beside work, or controls/status information that must remain continuously visible. Dolphin explicitly gives clocks, browser address bars and Excel formula bars as Hooked Area examples. citeturn8view3

Testing should compare region-level pinning against the simpler “keep this whole application on the reference monitor” alternative. The latter may remove considerable configuration complexity.

### Private Detail / Public Output

During presentation or screen sharing, the user retains a privately magnified notes/control/detail display while the presentation or shared output remains at ordinary scale.

SuperNova explicitly supports this presentation pattern. citeturn9view0

The evidence for *actual frequency* is not yet strong enough to justify building it, but the fit with three-screen Doxa hardware is unusual enough to merit concept testing with visually impaired professionals and trainers.

### Visual State

Rather than a large settings tree, users switch among a handful of explicit states such as **Normal, Reading, Detail, Overview, Meeting**. Each state may alter zoom, tracking and visual enhancements together.

This is not an incumbent feature copied directly. It is an inference from two patterns: AT products contain many separately configurable parameters, while PowerToys Workspaces shows that mainstream users already understand named task configurations. citeturn15view1turn2view0

The key test is whether states simplify real work or merely hide controls users need individually.

## Open-source implementation signal, technical research candidates, and decision answer

### Open-source and lighter-product signal

This was deliberately limited to answering **“does a practical implementation precedent exist?”**, not deciding architecture.

| Source | Implementation signal | Apparent complexity / dependency | Licensing / provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Microsoft PowerToys** | Find My Mouse, crosshairs, Mouse Jump/CursorWrap, FancyZones and Workspaces demonstrate cross-monitor cursor utilities and persistent workspace/layout concepts in a large current Windows codebase. citeturn15view0turn15view1turn15view2 | Medium overall; individual pointer utilities appear much narrower than full magnification. Workspace restoration has documented app-specific limitations. | Microsoft PowerToys' GitHub repository currently declares an **MIT licence**. |
| **HoverText** | A current Windows low-vision project implements local enlargement and enlarged text near the cursor, using UI Automation first, optional OCR fallback and pixel magnification. It offers multiple magnifier backends including GDI, the native Windows Magnification API and DirectX desktop duplication. | Useful proof that lightweight “inspect text/detail without full-screen zoom” is implementable. Its need for multiple fallback paths also demonstrates application-compatibility complexity. | Repository declares **MIT** and contains an MIT licence; it is an early-stage project, so existence should not be confused with production maturity. |
| **Low Vision Zoom** | Current Windows Store product deliberately centres its proposition on smooth magnification behaviour rather than incumbent breadth. citeturn24search6 | Useful UX signal, but implementation provenance is not publicly established by the Store material found. | Treat as product-behaviour evidence only; no source-code licensing conclusion should be inferred. |
| **Windows Magnification platform** | Windows itself and small Windows projects establish that desktop magnification is exposed through Windows facilities; HoverText explicitly lists the Windows Magnification API as one of its backends. | A single magnified view is demonstrably feasible; this tells us very little about robust independent triple-monitor magnification, focus semantics or capture/sharing behaviour. | Microsoft/API use needs separate technical/licensing review if selected. |
| **“Wind” / Magnifier+ references in the brief** | I did **not** find sufficiently unambiguous public provenance to identify the exact intended projects and verify the requested licensing boundary confidently. | **Research gap.** | They should not be treated as licensed implementation assets until exact repository identity and file-level provenance are established. |

The most important signal from open source is therefore **not “the code already exists”**. It is that three narrower ideas have known implementations: cross-monitor pointer finding, saved workspace organisation, and temporary/local magnification. What remains technically uncertain is the integration of those behaviours into a robust independent multi-display magnification environment.

### Technical research candidates

If the highest-value concepts survive user testing, no more than five technical questions deserve the next investigation:

**Independent display state:** Can Windows reliably produce genuinely independent magnified visual states on two and especially three extended monitors across mixed DPI/scaling, GPU configurations, full-screen applications and common Office/browser workloads? ZoomText's current two-display limitation makes this the first technical risk to investigate. citeturn4view0

**Point-of-Interest semantics:** How reliably can Visual detect and distinguish mouse pointer, text caret, keyboard/UI focus and application focus across Win32, UI Automation, browsers, Electron, Office and remote/virtual applications without generating intrusive viewport movement? The incumbent investment in separate tracking/enhancement systems shows that this is not one signal. citeturn4view1turn9view1

**Capture and presentation behaviour:** Can private magnification/visual enhancement be separated reliably from screen-share, presentation and external-output capture across Teams/Zoom/PowerPoint and Windows capture paths? SuperNova proves the workflow is commercially recognised, not how straightforward it is to reproduce. citeturn9view0

**Display topology and restoration:** How reliably can application positions, monitor roles and Visual state survive hot-plugging, docking, orientation changes, sleep/resume and mixed DPI? PowerToys Workspaces' documented positioning limitations indicate that even mainstream restoration has application-specific edge cases. citeturn15view1

**Performance envelope:** What capture/render/tracking latency is perceptually acceptable during continuous low-vision use, and what happens under GPU-heavy video meetings, browsers and Office workloads? The 2026 Windows Magnifier regression reports are anecdotal but underline that responsiveness needs to be treated as a first-class acceptance criterion. citeturn22search7

### Research gaps before product selection

Several questions remain too weakly evidenced for product commitment.

There is **no credible public distribution of real-world magnification levels** sufficient to tell Doxa whether most target users live at 1.5×, 2×, 4×, 8× or much higher. Vendor maximums cannot answer this. citeturn2view0turn7view0

There is insufficient direct evidence showing **how frequently low-vision professionals actually use Freeze View/Hooked Areas**, as opposed to those features simply appearing in mature products and training material. Dolphin's examples show plausible value, not usage frequency. citeturn8view3turn6search0

There is insufficient evidence to choose between **smooth animated zoom and near-instant stepped zoom**. Low Vision Zoom supplies a credible alternative design hypothesis but not yet a mature user evidence base. citeturn24search6turn24search12

There is insufficient evidence for the value of **per-monitor colour transformations** specifically, as distinct from colour/contrast enhancement generally.

And most importantly, there is little public evidence concerning **three-display low-vision workflows**. That is simultaneously a research weakness and the reason Doxa-specific testing matters: current ZoomText documentation stops at two extended monitors. citeturn4view0

### Final decision question

> **Which small set of Visual capabilities appears capable of delivering most of the commercial value, particularly on Doxa's compact multi-monitor hardware, without reproducing the complexity of ZoomText or SuperNova?**

The evidence currently concentrates around a **six-part value hypothesis**, rather than around broad magnifier feature parity:

**Independent per-monitor visual state** appears to be the structural differentiator. Existing users explicitly want one screen magnified while another remains normal, ZoomText has built specialised modes for exactly that purpose, and current ZoomText still limits extended-monitor support to two displays. citeturn3view1turn17search7turn17search19turn4view0

**Overview + Detail** appears to be the most important workflow built on top of that capability. It directly attacks context loss rather than trying to make panning progressively cleverer.

**Temporary enlargement + immediate return to context** has the strongest potential to extend Visual beyond traditional assistive-technology users to normal-vision users of 14–15in displays. The value proposition is not “I use magnification software”; it is “this particular thing is too small—show it clearly, then put me back”.

**Pointer/caret/focus finding, with restrained tracking**, addresses the second major cost of magnification: locating Point-of-Interest. The evidence argues for separate concepts of *highlight* and *follow*, not an all-or-nothing automatic tracking system. ZoomText and SuperNova already separate these targets; PowerToys shows that pointer finding has mainstream value as well. citeturn4view1turn9view1turn15view0

**Doxa monitor roles/workspace restoration** is where the hardware/software combination may become more commercially distinctive. PowerToys validates spatial workspaces for mainstream Windows users, while a Doxa system can go further by restoring not just window position but each screen's *visual purpose*. citeturn15view1turn15view2

Finally, **a small set of visual-enhancement states**—principally contrast/inversion/colour and highly visible pointer/caret/focus indicators—looks worthwhile, provided Visual resists reproducing decades of specialist settings before user testing demonstrates their value. citeturn8view1turn4view1

Pinned/reference areas and private presentation views remain credible second-order opportunities. Rich speech, exhaustive magnifier geometries, very high magnification targets, OCR-heavy reading systems and extensive application-specific rule engines currently have a much weaker **value-to-complexity fit for Visual**, even though several are genuinely useful to subsets of incumbent customers. citeturn8view3turn9view0turn6search13

The strategic distinction is therefore fairly sharp:

**ZoomText and SuperNova largely ask, “How do we make one magnified Windows desktop manageable?”**

The more promising Visual question is:

**“Given that Doxa already provides two or three compact physical viewports, how much magnification complexity can we eliminate by giving those screens stable, intelligible roles?”**
