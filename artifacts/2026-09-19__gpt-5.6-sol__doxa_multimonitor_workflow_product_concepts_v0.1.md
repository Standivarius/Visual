# Visual — Deep Research 03: Doxa Multi-Monitor Workflow and Product Concept Research

## Executive conclusion and evidence status

The evidence supports the central Doxa hypothesis, but with an important qualification:

> **The strongest opportunity is not “more magnification across more screens”. It is using physical screens to make context stable while detail changes.**

Conventional screen magnification creates an unavoidable field-of-view problem: as magnification rises, less of the underlying interface remains visible. Recent low-vision research continues to describe this as a detail-versus-context trade-off: users pan and zoom, reconstruct spatial context from memory, and can become disoriented when moving between detail and overview. A 2026 study with 22 low-vision participants found that providing simultaneous contextual information could reduce effort, but also found that adding visual context could increase visual load; its mini-map treatment was not uniformly preferred. That is an important warning against assuming that “another overview” is automatically useful. citeturn18view1turn25academia36

Doxa's potential advantage is therefore **spatial stability**. A 14–15 inch panel can be poor as a single all-purpose desktop because enlargement or Windows scaling consumes much of its logical workspace. Two or three such panels become more interesting if they are deliberately *not* treated as one giant desktop: one can remain a stable context, work, reference, or communication surface while another changes according to the user's immediate visual need. This is a product hypothesis derived from the low-vision context-loss evidence and from established multi-monitor patterns in specialist and mainstream software, rather than a demonstrated Doxa user outcome yet. citeturn18view1turn21view1turn21view2

There is also a surprisingly concrete competitive opening. Freedom Scientific's current ZoomText User Guide, published in 2026, documents Span, Clone, Zoom with 1x, MultiView Local and MultiView Global for multiple monitors. MultiView allows each display to have its own zoom level. However, the same current manual's troubleshooting section explicitly addresses systems with **three or more monitors** and instructs the user to disable displays until only one extended display remains; it says current multiple-monitor support works with two displays and that three-or-more support is for a future release. The wording uses “two extended displays” somewhat inconsistently, but the operational instruction is unambiguous: **ZoomText 2026's documented multi-monitor configuration is effectively two active displays, not a Doxa-style three-display extended desktop.** citeturn27view0turn27view3

This matters because ZoomText 2026 is not legacy software: Freedom Scientific lists a current 2026.2608 release dated August 12, 2026. citeturn22search2

ZoomText already validates several underlying workflows. Its current guide describes an unmagnified bird's-eye display alongside magnification, separate magnified views, Clone, Span and Freeze Views. “Zoom with 1x” is explicitly intended to combine a magnified work view with a normal overview, including collaboration between a low-vision and sighted user. citeturn27view0turn27view1 Dolphin's current 2026 training catalogue likewise continues to teach multiple-monitor extended desktop use and Hooked Areas; an older detailed SuperNova manual describes Span, side-by-side applications, cloned standard views and a presentation mode in which the user works magnified while other displays remain normal. Because that detailed manual is older, those interaction descriptions should be treated as historical/product-line evidence rather than proof of every current SuperNova implementation detail. citeturn5search0turn5search1turn6view2

The commercial lesson is **not** that Visual should recreate those modes. User reports show precisely why not. Low-vision ZoomText users repeatedly describe getting lost at higher magnification, unwanted viewport movement, performance issues, configuration burden and application compatibility problems. A 2025 r/Blind discussion contains both satisfied long-term users and users who found ZoomText slow, unreliable or difficult; one participant reported replacing it with Windows Magnifier despite losing independent two-screen zoom because the simpler setup was more productive for them. These are anecdotes, not prevalence estimates, but they expose an important trade-off: capability has value only while its behavioural complexity remains manageable. citeturn24view0turn24view1

**Bottom-line research judgment:** Visual should prototype **stable context/detail separation, reversible inspection, and role-based physical screens** before it prototypes sophisticated magnifier modes. Pointer/focus assistance should support those experiences, but is probably a baseline enabler rather than the main commercial proposition.

## Doxa workflow map and task model

**A. Doxa workflow map**

The three target populations should not receive identical behaviour.

| User group | Dominant problem | Best use of multiple compact screens | Poor fit / danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Moderate/severe low vision** | Magnification makes controls readable but removes surrounding context; finding pointer/caret/focus and navigating the enlarged viewport become substantial work. | Keep one working/detail surface highly readable while preserving context or reference elsewhere; minimise unnecessary movement of the stable display. Speech may complement magnification for some users. | An entirely unmagnified 14–15" “overview” may itself be unreadable; three actively moving magnified views could increase rather than reduce disorientation. citeturn18view1turn25academia36turn25search2 |
| **Mild visual difficulty** | Most UI is usable until small text, dense controls, fatigue, glare or a specific task demands enlargement. | Normal Windows-scaled workspace most of the time, with rapid inspection/enlargement, strong pointer/caret visibility and task-specific visual states. | Persistent full-screen magnification may solve a problem the user only has intermittently and can cost useful workspace. |
| **Normal vision** | Compact panels provide less physical display area per screen; moving windows/pointer between screens and reconstructing workspaces creates friction. | Stable app/monitor roles, workspace restoration, quick pointer movement/finding, fixed references and occasional enlargement of dense information. | Accessibility-style automatic tracking or conspicuous highlighting can feel intrusive; ordinary Windows scaling or a larger monitor may simply be better. Mainstream users already report annoyance with mismatched displays and cursor transitions. citeturn9search3turn9search7turn21view0 |

For the severe-low-vision group, the phrase **“overview monitor” should not be taken literally as “1× monitor.”** ZoomText itself distinguishes between multiple magnified views and its magnified-plus-1× mode, and its current MultiView modes support different zoom levels per display. That is a useful precedent: the stable-context screen might be 1× for one user, 1.5× for another and a high-contrast lightly enlarged view for another. citeturn27view0

### What compact panels change

The physical constraint is straightforward. Increasing Windows scaling on a 14–15" screen makes content physically easier to see but leaves fewer logical pixels for application content. Persistent magnification further restricts the visible region. Several small panels therefore do not automatically equal one large monitor: they supply **separate physical surfaces**, each of which can hold less information at a readable size.

That distinction is where Doxa can either win or lose.

A large 27–32" display is likely preferable when the user's main requirement is simply “show more of one application at a readable physical size”. An ultrawide is attractive when applications benefit from continuous horizontal space and the user does not need physical role boundaries. Some low-vision users explicitly prefer a physically large display or moving the display closer instead of navigating heavy screen magnification; one long-time low-vision user in an r/Blind discussion described exactly that preference. citeturn24view1 Research into low-vision video viewing likewise identifies reduced visible area as a fundamental consequence of magnification and has studied larger displays as an alternative way of recovering context. citeturn25search12

Doxa becomes more defensible when the workflow contains **two or three information streams with different visual stability requirements**. In that case physical separation can be an advantage rather than merely compensation for small screens.

### Task-by-task workflow

The following is a design synthesis from low-vision context research, ZoomText/SuperNova behaviour, PowerToys multi-monitor patterns and reported multi-monitor use. It should be tested rather than treated as established preference. citeturn18view1turn27view0turn6view2turn21view1

| Task | Information that should remain stable | Information needing frequent enlargement/change | Natural two-screen pattern | Useful third screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Word/document editing** | Paragraph/page structure or source material | Current sentence, toolbar/dialog, insertion point | Detail editor + context/reference | Outlook/Teams or separate source |
| **PDF reading** | Page position/thumbnails/document structure | Current text/diagram | Stable page context + enlarged reading view | Notes/reference application |
| **Browser/SaaS** | Navigation, app structure, reference data | Form field, small table, menus, dense controls | Work/detail + stable context | Communication/reference |
| **Outlook/email** | Message list/calendar/context | Current message or composer | Mail context + reading/writing detail | Related document or Teams |
| **Excel** | Row/column context, headings, formula/reference cells | Active cell/range, ribbon/dialog | Sheet overview + active-area detail | Source document/dashboard |
| **Teams/Zoom** | Other participants/chat/meeting state | Shared document or user's work | Meeting + magnified work | Private detail/notes is a credible third role |
| **Reference-based writing** | Source/reference | Current writing area | Work + reference | Overview/communication |
| **Presentation** | Audience output | User's magnified controls/notes | Private work + public output | Notes/communication/context |
| **Application switching** | Spatial identity of major applications | Current active task | Stable app roles | Third persistent task/reference |

The Excel case is particularly important. A magnifier can make a cell legible while simultaneously hiding row headings, column headings and distant formula dependencies. That is exactly the class of “detail destroys context” problem for which two physical surfaces have a credible structural advantage. The current ZoomText guide's own bird's-eye/magnified mode is evidence that the general need exists, although whether Doxa users prefer a stable whole-sheet view, a lightly magnified context or another arrangement remains untested. citeturn27view1turn18view1

Likewise, “Follow What Matters” should not start from the assumption that the viewport must move whenever the pointer, caret or keyboard focus moves. Freedom Scientific describes ZoomText as keeping the user's point of interest in view as the mouse moves, text is typed and applications are navigated. That convenience is valuable, but direct user reports also describe unexpected viewport snapping and the effort involved in constantly moving through magnified content. citeturn23search4turn24view1 The product question is therefore not **whether to track**, but **when visibility alone is sufficient and when movement is genuinely necessary**.

## Two-screen and three-screen models

**B. Two-screen model**

The evidence supports four credible arrangements. The first two deserve substantially more attention than the latter two.

| Dual-screen arrangement | Behaviour | Best users/tasks | Research judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Detail + Context** | One panel is the readable active view; the other remains a stable full or lightly magnified representation of the current application/desktop. | Moderate/severe low vision; Excel, PDFs, complex web apps, document navigation. | **Highest-priority dual-screen concept.** Directly attacks context loss rather than merely adding desktop space. ZoomText's Zoom-with-1x and independent MultiView behaviours validate the underlying need. citeturn27view0turn27view1 |
| **Work + Reference** | Main application stays on one display; source document, instructions, email, chat or dashboard remains on the other. Each screen can retain its own visual state. | All three groups; writing, research, email, workflows with instructions/checklists. | **Broadest commercial overlap.** Mainstream tools such as Workspaces/FancyZones already demonstrate demand for persistent application placement, though they do not provide Doxa-specific visual roles. citeturn21view1turn21view2 |
| **Private Detail + Public Output** | User sees magnified/private controls on one screen while the audience or screen-share receives a normal view from the second. | Low-vision presenters, trainers, teachers, meetings. | Real need, but lower-frequency. SuperNova's documented Presentation Mode is strong capability evidence; current ZoomText users still report screen-sharing friction, so compatibility risk is material. citeturn6view2turn26search13 |
| **Independent Work A + Work B** | Two applications each retain their own magnification/contrast level rather than one global visual state. | Users who alternate repeatedly between two applications. | Useful, but less differentiated unless state follows the physical monitor automatically. ZoomText already has separate per-display zoom in MultiView. citeturn27view0 |

For Doxa, **Detail + Context should not span one magnified viewport across both panels**. ZoomText's Span mode does precisely that, treating two monitors as one larger magnified view. That may suit some users, but it sacrifices the core Doxa hypothesis: the context surface should stop moving. citeturn27view1

This also suggests a negative design principle:

> When one screen is intentionally the user's orientation anchor, pointer movement, caret movement or application focus on the other screen should not casually disturb it.

**C. Three-screen model**

The third monitor is valuable only when it lets an information stream remain visible that would otherwise displace either work or context. “Three is better than two” is not supported as a general proposition.

| Triple-screen arrangement | First screen | Second screen | Third screen | What the third panel genuinely adds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Overview + Work + Reference** | Stable context | Magnified active work | Source/instructions/email | Probably the strongest low-vision triple-screen configuration: reference no longer has to replace overview. |
| **Work + Reference + Communication** | Main task | Source/data | Teams/Slack/email/video | Strong mild-vision and mainstream overlap; communication remains visible without stealing work space. User reports from mainstream multi-monitor communities commonly describe relegating chat/log/status material to secondary screens, though such anecdotes do not establish productivity gains. citeturn9search7 |
| **Private Detail + Public Output + Notes/Meeting** | Magnified user view | Clean audience/share view | Notes/chat/participants | Makes presentation and hybrid-meeting roles much cleaner than two screens because private magnification no longer competes with presenter notes or communications. SuperNova's older Presentation Mode shows the two-role need; the third-role extension is a Doxa inference. citeturn6view2 |
| **Detail + Context + Secondary Work** | Detail | Stable overview | Second application | Potentially useful for Excel/research-heavy users, but carries the greatest risk of excessive visual scanning. |

Three displays become counterproductive when the user has to **search three dynamic magnified views**, when all three compete for attention, or when cursor transitions become harder to predict. Mainstream users already report annoyance when monitor sizes, scaling or geometries make cursor movement behave unexpectedly; PowerToys' 2026 addition of CursorWrap and its existing Mouse Jump/Find My Mouse utilities show that long cursor travel and cursor location are sufficiently real interaction problems to attract mainstream tooling. citeturn9search3turn9search7turn21view0

There is also a hard competitive point: the current ZoomText manual explicitly documents a limitation with three or more monitors. Thus, a Doxa triple-screen workflow should **not** be designed around running ZoomText's existing multi-monitor modes underneath it and assuming all three screens can participate normally. citeturn27view0

For SuperNova, I found current 2026 training material for multi-monitor workflows but no comparably explicit current public statement establishing the maximum supported display count. Its detailed older manual refers to multiple “remaining” monitors in Presentation Mode, but that is insufficient to certify a 2026 three-display configuration. **Current SuperNova triple-monitor support therefore remains unverified in this study.** citeturn5search0turn6view2

### What the incumbent products teach

ZoomText's current design contains several valuable mental models but also illustrates the accumulation problem Visual should avoid. The 2026 guide offers Span, Clone, Zoom with 1x, MultiView Local, MultiView Global, Overview Mode, Freeze View and multiple-monitor configuration options. Freeze Views can be moved across monitors only under certain zoom-window conditions, and some other features are explicitly unsupported with multiple monitors. That is powerful software, but it exposes substantial mode knowledge to the user. citeturn27view0turn28view1

SuperNova similarly demonstrates legitimate workflows—Hooked Areas, extended desktops, presentation output, application separation—but configuration of a Hooked Area historically involved selecting an area, positioning and sizing it, choosing magnification and smoothing, and deciding whether it follows focus. That is exactly the sort of flexibility that is useful to a specialist and potentially excessive for someone whose requirement is merely “keep this reference visible over there”. citeturn5search5turn7view1

Windows Magnifier occupies the opposite end of the spectrum: it provides built-in Windows magnification with full-screen/lens/docked views and standard zoom controls, but does not offer the same specialist independent multi-monitor workflows. This simplicity is precisely why some users tolerate its limitations. A recent low-vision user explicitly reported switching to it despite losing different zoom levels on a two-screen setup because their overall work experience improved. That remains one person's experience, but it illustrates Visual's strategic danger: **adding a clever capability can reduce total usability.** citeturn13search0turn24view0

PowerToys provides another useful benchmark. Its current Mouse Utilities include Find My Mouse, configurable crosshairs, Mouse Jump across monitors and CursorWrap; Workspaces captures and relaunches application layouts; FancyZones manages physical-window placement and can rotate windows between monitors. Visual therefore should not treat ordinary cursor finding or ordinary window arrangement as proprietary differentiation. Its opportunity is to combine those ideas with **visual state and known physical monitor roles for low vision**. citeturn21view0turn21view1turn21view2

### Negative evidence that should change the design

Several findings argue against maximal automation.

First, simultaneous context can help but can also add clutter. The 2026 low-vision chart study found improved access from contextual assistance but also higher visual load; its mini-map concept was not the best treatment for every task. Thus a permanent overview display should be user-testable and dismissible, not treated as dogma. citeturn18view1

Second, viewport following can solve one problem while creating another. ZoomText's normal model follows mouse, typing and navigation, yet users report unwanted jumps and getting lost. Visual should therefore begin from **visibility first, movement second**: highlight the pointer/caret/focus clearly, move the detail viewport only when the point of interest would otherwise become unusable, and make manual override immediate. citeturn23search4turn24view1

Third, highly conspicuous pointer treatments should be optional. Experimental work on the Sunny Pointer showed a seven-fold improvement in a targeting task among participants with peripheral vision loss, which is unusually strong evidence for locator value—but the intervention used screen-spanning converging lines and was designed for a particular visual-field impairment. That does not justify forcing large crosshairs on every low-vision or mainstream user. citeturn25academia38

Fourth, performance and compatibility are commercial features, not engineering trivia. Current and historical user reports describe ZoomText performance, application crashes and compatibility difficulties; a 2024 Zotero case documents a reproducible conflict involving ZoomText/Fusion and a Word add-in, and a 2026 ZoomText user described display freezes and screen-share cursor problems. None establishes broad failure rates, but they make “keep the behavioural surface small” commercially sensible. citeturn24view0turn24view2turn26search13

## Prototypes, deferrals, and migration UX

**E. Best concepts for prototyping**

Five concepts merit prototype investment, but they are not equally important.

### Overview + Detail

**Target:** primarily moderate/severe low vision; secondarily users dealing with dense spreadsheets, PDFs or interfaces.

**Exact workflow:** the user works on a readable magnified view while a second screen preserves stable spatial context. Pointer, caret or focus can identify the corresponding location without continually moving the context screen. With three displays, the third panel can remain a genuine reference rather than forcing the overview to double as a reference surface.

**Why Doxa improves it:** two known physical displays make the distinction persistent and spatial: “this is where I work; that is where I orient myself.” Existing ZoomText “Zoom with 1x” already demonstrates the magnified + bird's-eye concept, while low-vision research confirms the underlying context-loss problem. citeturn27view1turn18view1

**Success:** users recover their place faster, pan/zoom less, and make fewer orientation errors than with a single moving magnified viewport.

**Invalidation:** users cannot meaningfully interpret the small overview; looking between screens creates more effort than zooming out; or users overwhelmingly prefer a large single monitor.

### Inspect and Return

**Target:** mild/moderate visual difficulty first, normal vision second, sustained magnification users for occasional inspection.

**Exact workflow:** invoke enlargement on the item under the mouse, caret or keyboard focus; inspect it; release or invoke one command; return **exactly** to the previous visual state and position.

The product should test two versions: enlargement on the current monitor and enlargement on a dedicated Doxa detail monitor. The latter is more differentiated because the primary display never loses context.

**Why Doxa improves it:** a second panel can act as a temporary optical instrument rather than forcing the working display itself to zoom. The lightweight Low Vision Zoom product is useful interaction evidence because it deliberately emphasizes smooth pointer-centred zoom that preserves place rather than a large feature set. citeturn16search12

**Success:** users inspect small UI/text without permanent Windows scaling changes, manual zoom-out or reorientation.

**Invalidation:** ordinary application/browser zoom or Windows Magnifier proves faster; dedicated-screen inspection requires excessive eye movement; or users routinely need sustained rather than temporary enlargement.

### Monitor Roles

**Target:** all three groups, particularly Doxa users who repeatedly dock/undock or work with the same application set.

**Exact workflow:** physical displays have understandable identities—typically **Work**, **Context/Reference**, and optionally **Communication**. Applications return to those places; each role may also retain its relevant zoom/contrast state.

The critical simplification is that users should not need to understand arbitrary monitor numbers, screen coordinates or a large layout editor.

**Why Doxa improves it:** unlike generic software, Doxa can know the normal physical topology of its own dual/triple configuration. PowerToys proves the adjacent mainstream need: Workspaces captures and restores applications, while FancyZones supplies custom spatial layouts and cross-monitor movement. It also proves the edge cases: Microsoft documents that some applications launch/reposition differently and that elevated applications may not reposition correctly. citeturn21view1turn21view2

**Success:** users reconnect the hardware and resume work without manually reconstructing application locations or accessibility settings.

**Invalidation:** Windows/PowerToys already solves restoration adequately for the target group, or application behaviour makes Doxa role restoration too unreliable.

### Find and Move

**Target:** low vision first; normal-vision triple-screen users second.

**Exact workflow:** one deliberate action visibly identifies the current pointer and current monitor; another can move it deliberately to the centre or last-known working position of a chosen screen. Boundary assistance could reduce accidental cross-screen escape but should not be mandatory.

**Why Doxa improves it:** physical left/centre/right destinations are known and can be named semantically rather than by Windows display number. PowerToys already supports pointer spotlight, crosshairs, Mouse Jump and CursorWrap, so Visual should aim for less configuration and tighter integration with Doxa monitor roles rather than duplicate those utilities. citeturn21view0

**Success:** users find and transfer the pointer without hunting or overshooting, particularly between three compact displays.

**Invalidation:** PowerToys/Windows is already satisfactory, locator effects distract users, or keyboard-centric low-vision users rarely need the feature.

### Follow What Matters — visibility-first variant

**Target:** sustained-magnification users.

**Exact workflow:** mouse pointer, text caret and keyboard focus are separate concepts. Visual makes the active signal easy to see. It moves the magnified detail view only when movement is necessary, and users can immediately suspend tracking.

For example, typing should not cause large repeated viewport movement simply because the caret approaches a threshold if a small reposition would suffice. Tab navigation should visibly identify focus before an abrupt magnified jump occurs.

**Why Doxa improves it:** the detail display can follow activity while the context/reference display remains stable.

**Success:** users complete keyboard, typing and mixed mouse/keyboard tasks while reporting less surprise and fewer manual corrections than with aggressive tracking.

**Invalidation:** users actually prefer conventional automatic following; delayed movement makes the interface slower; or reliable identification of the meaningful point of interest proves too inconsistent across applications.

**F. Concepts to defer or reject initially**

**Private Detail / Public Output should be deferred, not rejected.** Its use case is legitimate: SuperNova historically provides an explicit presentation arrangement, and a 2026 ZoomText user report shows that screen sharing still creates real accessibility edge cases. Three displays could make private detail + public output + notes unusually elegant. But the workflow affects a narrower portion of the working day and carries disproportionately high compatibility/support risk. It should follow core daily-work prototypes rather than lead them. citeturn6view2turn26search13

**Visual States should be treated as a later simplification layer, not a first independent feature.** “Reading”, “Work” or “Meeting” can be excellent labels once Visual knows which settings genuinely need bundling. Designing the modes first risks inventing categories users do not naturally recognise. Explicit switching should precede automatic state switching; otherwise Visual merely replaces dozens of visible settings with hidden automation.

**Clone and Span should not be priority Doxa concepts.** They solve real specialist cases, but Clone duplicates information rather than exploiting physical roles, while Span converts multiple displays into one large moving magnified canvas—the opposite of Visual's strongest hypothesis. ZoomText already provides both. citeturn27view1

**Arbitrary multi-region Freeze/Hooked-Area editors should be deferred.** An entire Doxa reference display may achieve most of the relevant value with much lower conceptual burden. ZoomText and SuperNova show that sophisticated regions are possible; they also show the configuration cost involved. citeturn26search3turn5search5

### Migration UX

**G. What should remain familiar**

Experienced ZoomText/SuperNova users should encounter familiar **concepts**, not copied interfaces:

| Preserve | Why |
|---|---|
| **Zoom + / − and an unmistakable 1×/Normal action** | Core magnifier vocabulary and a low-risk escape route; current ZoomText specifically exposes Zoom-to-1× alongside zoom controls. citeturn27view0 |
| **Pointer, caret and focus as distinct things** | Specialist products already train users to distinguish them; ZoomText exposes pointer, cursor and focus enhancements separately. citeturn23search22 |
| **Tracking on/off** | Necessary for users who know when automatic following helps or interferes. |
| **Color/contrast as a visual enhancement rather than an application setting** | Familiar AT model and useful across applications. |
| **Keyboard-first operation** | A long-time ZoomText user specifically described heavy reliance on hotkeys, and Freedom Scientific provides extensive keyboard commands and even a large-print keyboard built around quick ZoomText actions. citeturn24view1turn26search1 |
| **Overview/detail, magnified/1× and application-specific settings as concepts** | These map onto existing specialist mental models without requiring Visual to expose incumbent mode names. citeturn27view1 |

Visual should deliberately avoid asking users to choose among names equivalent to **Span / Clone / MultiView Local / MultiView Global / Zoom-with-1x / Freeze configuration** before accomplishing an ordinary work task. The goal should be phrased in user intent: “Keep this screen stable”, “Show this larger”, “Return”, “Find pointer”, “Send this window to Reference”.

That simplification is not cosmetic. An older AT practitioner discussion reported difficulty teaching ZoomText because of the volume of options, while users in the same discussion described having to discover which configurations worked for them over time. Because this evidence is from 2018, it should not be interpreted as a judgement on current ZoomText UX; it does, however, remain a useful warning about mature-product configuration depth. citeturn24view1

## Competitive gap and first prototype programme

**H. What can Visual plausibly do better because Doxa controls a known two-/three-display hardware configuration?**

The answer is **not magnification quality alone**. ZoomText and SuperNova have decades of specialist functionality, Windows provides a free baseline, and PowerToys already gives mainstream users sophisticated cursor and window utilities. citeturn23search0turn5search0turn21view0turn21view1

The plausible gap is to treat physical displays as **purposeful visual instruments rather than generic Windows coordinates**.

Visual can potentially know that a user's physical arrangement is, for example:

**left = Context, centre = Work, right = Reference**

and preserve that meaning across magnification, application placement, pointer movement and reconnection. Generic magnifiers must accommodate arbitrary PCs and arbitrary monitor topologies; generic window managers know where windows go but have no reason to understand a low-vision user's visual-state relationship between them.

That enables several simplifications.

A user could enlarge something without destroying the screen they were using to orient themselves. A pointer transfer could say “go to Reference” rather than “go to display 3”. A Work monitor could retain 2× magnification and pointer enhancement while Reference stays at 1.25× with different contrast. A triple-display Doxa system could keep Overview, Detail and Reference simultaneously available—something ZoomText 2026's own current guide does not document as supported because its multiple-monitor mode is limited to two active displays. citeturn27view0

The strongest commercial proposition therefore looks less like:

> “A simpler ZoomText.”

and more like:

> **“A multi-screen workspace where enlargement no longer makes you lose the rest of your work.”**

That proposition has a credible accessibility core **and** a mainstream extension. The same role architecture can become Work + Reference + Communication for someone with normal vision, while the visual states within those roles are much more important to a low-vision user.

This distinction matters. Normal-vision users do not need caret tracking or specialist magnification. Low-vision users do. The shared product layer is therefore **stable spatial roles and fast reversible transitions**, not an assumption that both populations require the same accessibility features.

**I. First prototype programme**

Only three behavioural prototypes are needed to answer most of the remaining product question.

### Stable Context + Detail

**User experience:** one display is explicitly designated Context and remains stable. A second is Detail. The user can select whether Detail follows the mouse, caret, keyboard focus or a manually chosen location, and can suspend following immediately. The context surface may be 1× or lightly magnified rather than obligatorily unmagnified.

**Test scenarios:** Excel navigation with row/column dependencies; Word/PDF editing while maintaining page position; browser/SaaS forms with menus and contextual information elsewhere on the page.

**Success criteria:** versus ordinary single-viewport magnification, participants spend less time zooming/panning to re-establish context, recover from navigation faster, and prefer the arrangement for at least one sustained work task. A failure is equally informative: if users repeatedly ignore the Context screen or find switching gaze between screens more burdensome, the central Doxa hypothesis weakens substantially.

### Inspect + Return

**User experience:** one command enlarges the item beneath mouse/caret/focus, either locally or on the second display. Releasing/invoking the command restores the exact prior display and workspace state. No modal setup is required.

**Test scenarios:** inspect a small toolbar control, spreadsheet cell/formula, PDF footnote, browser table, email detail and image; repeat while switching applications.

**Success criteria:** participants can inspect and return without manually correcting zoom or position, use it spontaneously after brief exposure, and mild-vision/normal-vision participants find it preferable to permanent scaling for intermittent needs.

### Role-based Doxa Workspace

**User experience:** users choose simple physical roles such as Work + Reference for two displays and Overview + Work + Reference or Work + Reference + Communication for three. Applications and visual settings belong to roles. A deliberate “Find/Move” action identifies the current pointer location and sends it to a named role.

**Test scenarios:** disconnect/reconnect the Doxa hardware; resume a research-writing workspace; move between Word/browser/email/Teams; repeat with two displays and then three.

**Success criteria:** users reconstruct the intended workspace with materially fewer manual window/visual adjustments, correctly understand the screen roles without remembering monitor numbers, and can explain what the third display contributes. If users cannot articulate a useful third role, that is a strong signal that the triple-monitor proposition should not be forced.

These three prototypes intentionally absorb several of the eight concepts without implementing them as eight separate “features”: Stable Context + Detail tests Overview + Detail and visibility-first Follow What Matters; Inspect + Return tests reversible magnification; Role-based Workspace tests Monitor Roles, Fixed Reference and Find/Move. Private/Public Output and full Visual States can wait.

## Research gaps and final decision

Several questions remain evidence gaps rather than reasons to add more desk research.

The most important is **what a useful “overview” physically looks like on a 14–15" panel for different levels and types of low vision**. Existing research establishes context loss, but it does not establish that Doxa's compact overview panel is large enough to solve it. The 2026 study's mixed results for mini-map-style context make this a genuine empirical risk, not a detail to settle by design preference. citeturn18view1

Second, the right balance between **pointer/caret/focus highlighting and automatic detail movement** needs direct comparative testing. Existing products prove that tracking is useful, while user reports prove that unwanted movement can be disruptive; the optimum threshold is task- and user-dependent. citeturn23search4turn24view1

Third, **the commercial value of three screens over two is not yet established for low vision**. It is strongest conceptually when the third surface preserves Reference or Communication without sacrificing Context, but this study found little direct low-vision user evidence for sustained three-screen workflows. That should remain an explicit research gap rather than be filled with extrapolation.

Fourth, current ZoomText three-monitor status is now reasonably clear: the 2026 Freedom Scientific guide itself describes three-or-more monitors as unsupported by the documented multiple-monitor configuration and tells users to reduce the active extended configuration. citeturn27view0 By contrast, **current SuperNova maximum-monitor support remains unresolved from the authoritative public material reviewed**; Dolphin's 2026 training confirms multiple-monitor workflows but does not provide the same explicit current display-count statement. citeturn5search0turn5search4

Finally, current user reports continue to show that reliability and predictability can outweigh raw feature count. In one recent discussion, users disagreed sharply: some liked ZoomText and pointed out that correct configuration solved apparent problems; others moved to simpler Windows Magnifier because of perceived performance or reliability. That disagreement should be preserved. Visual's opportunity is not based on the premise that specialist products are bad; it is based on the narrower proposition that **a known compact multi-display system may allow a simpler interaction model for a subset of their users.** citeturn24view0

### Final decision answer

If Visual were allowed to build only **three user experiences first**, the evidence points to:

| Priority | Experience | Why it survives the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | **Stable Context + Detail** | It addresses the defining magnification problem—loss of context—and is intrinsically stronger on two physical displays. It is supported by low-vision research and by established specialist magnifier behaviour, while still leaving a genuinely different Doxa simplification opportunity. citeturn18view1turn27view1 |
| **Second** | **Inspect and Return** | It has the widest bridge from accessibility into mainstream compact-screen use. It can help someone who needs magnification continuously, occasionally or merely for dense UI, while avoiding permanent loss of workspace. Lightweight magnifier design already supplies useful evidence for simple pointer-centred interaction. citeturn16search12 |
| **Third** | **Role-based Work / Reference / Communication workspace** | This is the strongest Doxa-specific commercial differentiator and the clearest route to normal-vision value. Mainstream software validates workspace restoration and multi-monitor window roles; Visual can add visual state and semantic physical roles. It also gives the third display a reason to exist rather than merely providing “more screen”. citeturn21view1turn21view2 |

**Find and Move should probably be built alongside those experiments as enabling hygiene rather than treated as one of the three headline propositions.** The need is real—PowerToys and low-vision research both support it—but generic pointer locating, crosshairs and monitor jumping are already available, so by themselves they offer weak commercial differentiation. citeturn21view0turn25academia38

The hardest conclusion is also the most useful: **the third Doxa display is not inherently an accessibility advantage.** It becomes one only when it can hold a stable third role that the user genuinely needs. Otherwise it adds another surface to search, another boundary for the pointer to cross and another source of visual attention. The first prototypes should therefore test not “how do we use all three screens?” but **“when does keeping something permanently somewhere else eliminate enough navigation to justify another screen?”**

That is the point at which Visual can plausibly capture much of the commercial value without becoming another ZoomText or SuperNova: **fewer modes, fewer moving views, stable physical meaning, and immediate reversibility.**
